Chord charts

Neck standing upright facing you. Thick top line = nut. Thickest string (low E) on the left.

Numbers = fingers (1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring). ○ = play string open. × = don't play that string.

Check every chord

Hold the shape and pick each string slowly, thick to thin. Every string without an × should ring clearly. Buzz means press closer to the fret. A dead thud means a finger is touching the next string, so arch your fingers and use the very tips.

One-minute changes

Pick two chords. Switch back and forth for 60 seconds, counting each change. Log your score and beat it tomorrow. Target: 30+.

Accelerators

  • Anchor fingers. Keep any finger that stays on the same string between chords pressed down or sliding, instead of lifting it.
  • Move as a unit. Lift your whole hand and land all fingers at once, not one by one.
  • Never stop the strumming hand. If a change is late, strum open strings and keep the beat.
  • Air changes. Practise chord shapes on your forearm while watching TV.
  • Record weekly. A 30-second phone clip each week shows progress on slow days.

Quick fixes

ProblemFix
A string buzzesPress right behind the fret wire, not in the middle of the gap.
A string sounds deadArch fingers more; a finger pad is touching the string below.
Chord sounds "off"Check tuning, then check you're not strumming × strings.
Hand crampsLoosen grip. You should be able to wiggle your thumb.
Sore fingertipsNormal for ~2 weeks. Shorter sessions, but don't skip days.
Changes too slowMore one-minute changes; look for anchor fingers.

Strumming pattern

D   D U   U D U

Count "1, 2 &, & 4 &". Hand moves down-up constantly like a pendulum and just misses the strings on the silent beats.
